## 3. 附录 A–J 的写作设计

### A. Data sources, variables and analysis samples

**中心问题：** 使用了什么数据，分析单位、变量和样本分别是什么？

- A.1 来源、研究区域、时期与原因分组。
- A.2 变量定义、单位、来源、变换及参考水平。
- A.3 从事件总表到不同分析样本的流转和缺失处理。
- A.4 已有协变量分布与 VIF；原因构成随阵风变化的描述结果。

**成型结果：** 变量字典、样本流转、原因映射/构成表；分布和 VIF 以读者需要为准合并呈现。

**不重复：** A 交代“哪些记录进入分析”；B 交代“一个事件的结果怎样计算”。正文 Table 1 不原样复制。

**来源：** 最终数据字典、样本汇总和已经核实的原因代码输出。本地取得最终版本，不从旧计划拷贝数字作为最终值。

### B. Event reconstruction and recovery-sample definition

**中心问题：** 多阶段监管记录如何成为一个工程含义清楚的事件？

- B.1 客户数聚合与再中断处理，实际起止跨度定义。

- B.3 零客户记录的已有特征及恢复样本排除理由。
- B.4 客户加权时长与事件跨度代表的不同量，以及采用当前定义的理由。

**成型结果：** 构造规则/简短伪代码、关键清理证据表；已有的清理前后统计。

**解释：** 从规则和已有证据说明定义。共享客户权重本身不能替代对“伪相关”的论证；没有运营字段支持时，不把短时恢复峰直接写成已确认的自动重合闸。

- FORMAT：结果已存在，缺表格、图或统一格式。

- MISSING：确实没有支撑该主张的结果。
- CLOSED：用户决定停止或不开展，不能自动重开。

## 5. 论文级呈现规则

每个附录小节依次写：目的（为什么需要）→ 方法（实际怎样做）→ 结果（表图显示什么）→ 对正文结论的作用。通常用短段落即可，不把审查日志改写成论文。

- 英文附录与正文一致；中文工作台账独立保存。
- 表图说明应包括分析单位、样本/时期、模型、预测来源、单位和必要缩写。
- 估计参数、原尺度反变换、对数尺度拟合量要分清；只解释实际采用的方法，不新增校正研究。
- 内部运行ID、Windows路径、检查项数量等留在结果登记表，不写进读者正文；论文复现说明使用仓库相对路径。
- 先分配稳定语义ID（如 `J_TIME_METRICS`），再按本地/目标格式产生显示编号。默认分附录编号 Table J1、Figure J1；已有统一 S 编号时沿用，不维护两套冲突编号。
- 主文保留关键结果和必要限制，不能把决定解释有效性的条件全部埋入附录。
- 不自动把所有17张 DD-TIME01 图加入论文。默认重点四面板；其余六任务结果在完整主表和必要说明中保留，原图在代码/结果材料中可查。
- 不把 negative controls、robustness 等标签用于不符合该设计的探索。使用 alternative specifications、sensitivity analyses、temporal evaluation 等准确称谓。

### J.3 Sensitivity to daily gust aggregation

Five daily summaries were compared using the same hourly reanalysis records for 111 districts over 1 April 2021–31 March 2024. The resulting panel contained 121,656 district-days. The weather locations and returned grid cells, outcome definitions, observation weights, model form and district-grouped folds were held fixed. The candidates were the daily maximum, the mean of hourly gust values, the 90th percentile, the mean of the three highest hourly values, and the largest mean over a consecutive three-hour window within the day. The last two summaries differ because the three highest values need not occur consecutively. The daily mean is a mean of hourly gust values, not a measure of sustained wind speed.

For the primary outcome, at least one incident affecting more than 100 customers, the daily maximum gave the lowest pooled out-of-fold Brier score, 0.05601044. Scores for the daily mean, 90th percentile, top-three mean and rolling three-hour mean were 0.05633271, 0.05610518, 0.05603522 and 0.05603435, respectively. The daily maximum also had the lowest score for the corresponding weather-attributed outcome, 0.01898542. The top-three and rolling summaries gave small improvements for the two outcomes exceeding 1,000 customers, but their paired conditional intervals included zero. These comparisons favour the daily maximum for the principal outcomes within the tested set; they do not establish its superiority to all possible weather summaries.

Calibration did not rank all candidates in the same order as the Brier score. Some apparent differences in binned calibration were strongly influenced by sparsely populated high-probability bins. The complete comparisons therefore retain both prediction scores and bin support. These analyses concern the independently sampled reanalysis exposure and do not validate the incident-dependent construction of the main-text proxy.

### J.4 Incremental contribution of duration-sensitive indicators

Two additional models evaluated whether information related to persistent strong gusts improved prediction beyond the daily maximum. Let g_h denote the hourly gust, G its daily maximum, and τ the 90th percentile of hourly gust values in the training data. Define

\[
H=\sum_{h=1}^{24}\mathbb{1}(g_h>\tau),\qquad
J=\frac{\sum_{h=1}^{24}(g_h^2-\tau^2)_+}{24\tau^2}.
\]

H counts hourly gust records exceeding the threshold; J combines exceedance intensity and persistence. With equally spaced hourly records, J is dimensionless. It is not a measurement of structural damage or dissipated wind energy. The training threshold was approximately 13.5–13.7 m/s across the district-grouped folds and 13.6 m/s for the full sample; it was not interpreted as a physical failure threshold.

The three models used p=p₀+(1−p₀)Φ(z), with

\[
z_0=(\ln G-\ln\theta)/\beta,\quad
z_1=z_0+\gamma H/24,\quad
z_2=z_0+\gamma\ln(1+J).
\]

Each augmented model re-estimated all its parameters within the training data, with γ unrestricted in sign. For the primary outcome, pooled Brier scores were 0.05601044 for the daily-maximum baseline, 0.05601130 after adding the exceedance-hour proportion, and 0.05601881 after adding the cumulative indicator. Corresponding scores for the weather-attributed outcome were 0.01898542, 0.01898574 and 0.01898814. The differences were small and the paired conditional intervals for these four comparisons included zero. The cumulative indicator produced small improvements for the two outcomes exceeding 1,000 customers, which are retained in the complete results.

The comparisons do not show a clear incremental benefit for the principal outcomes under these definitions and model forms. They do not imply that wind duration is physically irrelevant. The paired intervals resampled districts with the out-of-fold predictions held fixed and do not include the full uncertainty from refitting, model selection or dependence across districts on shared storm dates.

### J.5 Retrospective temporal evaluation of the main proxy model

The main-text district-day model was evaluated on a fixed later period without re-estimating its parameters. Development data covered 1 April 2021–29 September 2023, comprising 101,232 district-days across 111 districts. Evaluation data covered 30 September 2023–31 March 2024, comprising 20,424 district-days in the same districts. Each of the eight background-rate lognormal models was fitted using development data only. Parameters, constant-rate benchmarks and calibration-bin boundaries were frozen before evaluation. The benchmark for each outcome was its development-period event rate.

The evaluation is retrospective: the later period had already featured in earlier research exploration, and the weather proxy retained its same-day incident-anchor construction. It therefore assesses transfer to a later period under the same retrospective input definition, rather than pre-event forecasting.

All eight models had lower evaluation-period Brier scores than their development-rate benchmarks. For the primary outcome, the model and benchmark scores were 0.05960912 and 0.06147093, respectively, corresponding to a Brier skill score of 0.0303. For the weather-attributed outcome exceeding 100 customers, the corresponding scores were 0.02263666 and 0.02447269, giving a skill score of 0.0750. These are relative reductions in mean squared probability error of 3.03% and 7.50% against the specified benchmarks.

Improved scores did not imply uniform calibration. For the primary outcome, the overall mean prediction was 6.36%, compared with an observed rate of 6.58%. Predictions understated the January peak and overstated rates in February and March. January accounted for approximately 60% and 65% of the net Brier improvements, respectively, while November and December also contributed positively. Both models improved on the benchmark in five of the six complete months. The September observation covers only 30 September and is not interpreted as a complete month.

Evaluation gusts lay within the development-period range, although their distribution shifted towards higher values. The results show that the frozen models retained predictive information in the later period, with remaining risk-stratified and monthly calibration errors. They do not establish unchanged relationships across future years or networks.
